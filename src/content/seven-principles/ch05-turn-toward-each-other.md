## Summary

Romance is kept alive through small, everyday moments — not grand gestures. Your partner regularly makes **bids** for your attention, affection, or connection. A bid can be as subtle as a sigh, a question about your day, or reaching for your hand.

You can respond to a bid in three ways:

- **Turn toward** — engage with the bid ("What's on your mind?")
- **Turn away** — ignore the bid (keep scrolling your phone)
- **Turn against** — respond with hostility ("Can't you see I'm busy?")

Gottman's research found that couples who divorced within six years of their wedding had turned toward each other only **33%** of the time. Couples still together turned toward each other **86%** of the time. These small moments of connection add up to create your **emotional bank account** — the reservoir of goodwill that sustains your relationship through conflict and hard times.

## Practice: The Stress-Reducing Conversation

This is a **daily ritual** — 15 to 20 minutes per person, taking turns. The purpose is to help each other decompress from the day. This is *not* a time to discuss problems in your relationship.

### Rules

1. **Take turns.** Each person gets uninterrupted time to talk about their day.
2. **Don't give unsolicited advice.** Understanding must precede advice. Your job is to listen, not fix.
3. **Show genuine interest.** Make eye contact. Put your phone away. Don't let your mind wander.
4. **Communicate your understanding.** Reflect back what you've heard: "So you felt frustrated when..."
5. **Take your partner's side.** You are allies, not adversaries. Even if your partner's boss might have had a point, right now your partner needs solidarity.
6. **Express a "we against others" attitude.** "That sounds really unfair. What can we do about it?"
7. **Express affection.** Hold hands. Touch your partner's arm. Show physical warmth.
8. **Validate emotions.** Let your partner know their feelings make sense: "Of course you'd feel that way."

### Why it works

This conversation isn't about solving problems — it's about being there. When you consistently show up as a safe harbor for your partner's stress, you make massive deposits into the emotional bank account.

## Exercise: When Your Spouse Doesn't Turn Toward You

Sometimes a bid for connection gets missed or rebuffed. When that happens, use the questions below to process what you felt — separately first, then share with each other.

**Both partners answer:**

1. **What did you feel?**
   *(sad, lonely, angry, hurt, misunderstood, dismissed, unimportant...)*

2. **What triggered those feelings?**
   *(What specific moment or interaction set this off?)*

3. **Those recent feelings came from...**
   - A previous relationship
   - Family of origin experiences
   - Basic fears (rejection, abandonment, not being enough)
   - Unresolved issues between us
   - Unrealized hopes or dreams
   - Other: ___

4. **My contribution to this was...**
   *(What role did you play? Were you unclear in your bid? Were you already upset about something else?)*

5. **How can I make it better?**
   *(What can you personally do to repair this moment?)*

6. **What one thing can my partner do differently next time?**
   *(Be specific and actionable.)*

The goal is not blame — it's understanding. When you can name what happened and why it stung, you give your partner a map for turning toward you next time.

## Quick Review

- Romance lives in the **small moments**, not the grand gestures.
- Every bid for connection is a chance to **turn toward** your partner.
- Couples who stay together turn toward each other **86%** of the time.
- Your **emotional bank account** is built through daily deposits of attention, interest, and affection.
- The **Stress-Reducing Conversation** is one of the most powerful daily rituals you can adopt.
- When bids get missed, **process it together** — name the feeling, own your part, ask for what you need.
