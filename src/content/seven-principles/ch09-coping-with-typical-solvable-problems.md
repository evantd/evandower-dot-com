This chapter applies the problem-solving skills from Chapter 8 to six common relationship flashpoints. For each, Gottman identifies a core task, common issues, and practical solutions. All the skills from Chapter 8 — softened startup, repair attempts, self-soothing, compromise, tolerance — apply here. If compromise still feels impossible after working through these, the issue may be perpetual rather than solvable (see Chapter 10).

## 1. Work Stress

**Your task:** Make your marriage a place of peace and support.

**Common issues:** Bringing stress home, snapping at your partner, being emotionally unavailable after a hard day.

**What helps:**

- Create a transition ritual between work and home — give yourself time to wind down before engaging.
- Practice the Stress-Reducing Conversation daily (see Ch. 5). Take turns: one person talks about their day, the other listens and supports.
- Don't take your partner's stress reactions personally. Their irritability is about work, not you.
- Have regular "whining sessions" where each partner can vent while the other just listens supportively.
- Remember: you're allies, not adversaries. Your job is to help each other cope with the outside world.

## 2. In-Laws and Extended Family

**Your task:** Establish a sense of "we-ness" and solidarity as a couple.

**Common issues:** Loyalty conflicts between your spouse and your family of origin. In-laws competing with your spouse for your attention or allegiance. Boundary violations.

**What helps:**

- The partner whose family is causing tension must be the one to establish adult boundaries. Don't make your spouse fight your family for you.
- Convey to your family that your spouse comes first — you are now a new family unit.
- Side with your spouse when there's a conflict with your parents, even if you think your spouse is partially wrong. Work it out privately later.
- Create your own traditions and customs together. You're building a new family, not just joining an existing one.
- Communicate respect for in-laws while maintaining your couple boundary. It's not either/or.

## 3. Money

**Your task:** Balance security and pleasure, long-term and short-term needs.

**Common issues:** Different spending philosophies, hidden spending, disagreements about saving vs. spending, financial stress and anxiety.

**What helps:**

- Itemize your current expenditures together — get on the same page about what's actually happening.
- List each partner's financial worries and dreams. Understand what money means to each of you.
- Find common ground between your two approaches. There's usually overlap if you look for it.
- Decide on a workable strategy and agree to revisit the plan regularly. Financial plans need updating.
- Plan your financial future together: imagine life 5, 10, 20, 30 years out. What do you both want?

## 4. Sex

**Your task:** Communicate openly about your sexual needs and desires.

**Common issues:** Different levels of desire, feeling rejected, mismatched preferences, lack of physical intimacy.

**What helps:**

- Each partner should talk about what they find pleasurable — be specific. Vague hints don't work.
- Use softened startup when discussing sexual dissatisfaction. This is a vulnerable topic.
- Accept that sexual needs change over time. What worked five years ago may not work now.
- The inner/outer circles exercise from Chapter 8 works well for sexual issues too. Identify your non-negotiables vs. areas of flexibility.
- Don't take sexual differences personally. Different preferences aren't rejection.
- Maintain emotional intimacy — good sex flows from a strong friendship. The first three principles (love maps, fondness/admiration, turning toward) are the foundation.

## 5. Housework

**Your task:** Create a fair, agreed-upon division of labor.

**Common issues:** One partner (often the wife) feeling overburdened. Resentment building over unequal workloads. Different standards for "clean."

**What helps:**

- Gottman's research finding: when men do more housework, wives are happier AND the couple's sex life improves. Wives' heart rates during conflicts are measurably lower when they feel the domestic load is shared.
- List all household chores — everything, including the invisible ones.
- Compare the current division to your ideal division. Where are the gaps?
- Trade tasks you hate for ones you mind less. Play to your preferences.
- Do some chores together as bonding time.
- The specifics matter less than both partners feeling like equal contributors. Perception of fairness is what counts.

## 6. Becoming Parents

**Your task:** Expand your sense of "we" to include your children while maintaining your couple bond.

**Common issues:** Dramatic lifestyle changes, sleep deprivation, vastly reduced couple time, shifting roles and identities, unequal childcare burden.

**What helps:**

- Focus on your marital friendship — love maps, fondness and admiration, turning toward. These are your lifeline during the transition.
- Don't exclude the father from childcare. Both parents should be involved from the very start.
- Accept that the transition is genuinely hard. Give yourselves grace. You're not failing; you're adjusting.
- Carve out couple time, even if it's small. Fifteen minutes of real connection matters.
- Couples who have detailed love maps before the baby arrives cope significantly better with the transition. Know each other deeply before everything changes.
