## Summary

Marriages where one partner refuses to share power have an **81% chance** of self-destructing. In Gottman's research, the biggest challenge was men's willingness to accept influence from their wives — women already tend to do this naturally.

**Emotional intelligence** in a relationship means learning to yield, genuinely considering your partner's opinions, and searching for common ground. Accepting influence doesn't mean you never have an opinion or always give in. It means you treat your partner's perspective as worthy of serious consideration.

When men accept their wives' influence, wives soften their startup, which reduces conflict. The happiest, most stable marriages are those where both partners are willing to be influenced by each other. Refusing to share power isn't strength — it's a predictor of divorce.

## Questionnaire: Accepting Influence

Read each statement and answer **True** or **False**.

1. I am really interested in my partner's opinions on our basic issues.
2. I usually learn a lot from my partner even when we disagree.
3. I want my partner to feel that what he or she says really counts with me.
4. I generally want my partner to feel influential in this relationship.
5. I can listen to my partner, but only up to a point.
6. My partner has a lot of basic common sense.
7. I try to communicate respect even during our disagreements.
8. If I keep trying to convince my partner, I will eventually win out.
9. I don't reject my partner's opinions out of hand.
10. My partner is not rational enough to take seriously when we discuss our issues.
11. I believe in lots of give and take in our discussions.
12. I am very persuasive and usually can win arguments with my partner.
13. I feel I have an important say when we make decisions.
14. My partner usually has good ideas.
15. My partner is basically a great help as a problem solver.
16. I try to listen respectfully, even when I disagree.
17. My ideas for solutions are usually much better than my partner's.
18. I can usually find something to agree with in my partner's position.
19. My partner is usually too emotional.
20. I am the one who needs to make the major decisions in this relationship.

**Scoring:** Give yourself **1 point for each "True"** — except for questions **5, 8, 10, 12, 17, 19, and 20**, which are reversed (subtract 1 point for each "True" on those).

- **6 or above** = You do a good job of accepting influence.
- **Below 6** = You need to work on letting your partner influence you more.

## Exercise: The Gottman Island Survival Game

This is a fun, low-stakes way to practice sharing power and decision-making as a team.

### The Scenario

Your cruise ship has sunk in the Caribbean. You and your partner have washed up on a tropical desert island. One of you is injured. A storm is approaching. Items from the ship have washed ashore, but you can only carry **10 items** to safety.

### Step 1: Individual Rankings

Each partner **independently** selects and ranks 10 items from the Ship's Inventory below (1 = most important, 10 = least important of your picks).

### Step 2: Consensus

Now come together and agree on **one shared list** of 10 items, ranked. You must work as a team — no bullying, no steamrolling, no sulking.

### Step 3: Evaluate

After finishing, each partner answers the evaluation questions below.

### Ship's Inventory

1. Two changes of clothing
2. AM-FM and short-wave radio receiver
3. Ten gallons of water
4. Pots and pans
5. Matches
6. Shovel
7. Backpack
8. Toilet paper
9. Two tents
10. Two sleeping bags
11. Knife
12. Small life raft, with sail
13. Sunblock lotion
14. Cookstove and lantern
15. Long rope
16. Two walkie-talkie sender-receiver units
17. Freeze-dried food for seven days
18. One change of clothing
19. One fifth of whiskey
20. Flares
21. Compass
22. Regional aerial maps
23. Gun with six bullets
24. Fifty packages of condoms
25. First-aid kit with penicillin
26. Oxygen tanks

### Evaluation

Answer each question by choosing: **(a)** Not at all **(b)** Neither / A little **(c)** Somewhat **(d)** Very much

1. How effective were you at influencing your partner?
2. How effective was your partner at influencing you?
3. Did either of you try to dominate or were you competitive?
4. Did you sulk or withdraw?
5. Did your partner sulk or withdraw?
6. Did you have fun?
7. Did you work well as a team?
8. How much irritability or anger did you feel?
9. How much irritability or anger did your partner feel?
10. Did you both feel included?

**Scoring:** Give 1 point for **(a)**, 2 for **(b)**, 3 for **(c)**, 4 for **(d)**.

- For questions **3, 4, 5, 8, 9**: reverse the scoring (4 for "a", 3 for "b", 2 for "c", 1 for "d") — because *less* of those things is better.
- **Over 24** = You worked well as a team. You're good at sharing power.
- **24 or below** = You have room to grow in accepting each other's influence.

## Quick Review

- Refusing to share power predicts divorce with **81% accuracy**.
- Accepting influence ≠ having no opinions. It means genuinely considering your partner's perspective.
- **Yield to win**: Finding the reasonable part of your partner's complaint builds trust and opens dialogue.
- Practice sharing power in **low-stakes situations** (like a survival game) so it becomes natural in high-stakes ones.
- The happiest marriages are those where **both** partners accept influence from each other.
