## Summary

A **Love Map** is your mental model of your partner's inner world — their worries, joys, dreams, and daily details. You store everything you know about your partner in this cognitive space: who their friends are, what stresses them out, what they're hoping for, what happened at work today.

Couples with detailed love maps navigate life transitions — becoming parents, career changes, retirement — without losing their connection. When you know your partner deeply, you have a foundation that absorbs the shocks. Without this knowledge, stress drives you apart. You become strangers sharing a house.

The key insight: **knowing your partner is not a one-time event.** People change. Your partner's inner world shifts constantly. Happy couples keep updating their love maps — they ask questions, they notice, they stay curious. This ongoing attention to each other's world is the bedrock of friendship in marriage.

## Love Map 20 Questions Game

This is a game you play together. Here's how it works:

1. Each of you independently picks **20 numbers** between 1 and 60 (randomly — don't read the questions first).
2. Take turns. On your turn, read the question that matches your number.
3. Your partner tries to answer the question *about you*.
4. **If they get it right:** they earn the points shown in parentheses, and you earn 1 point (for knowing them well enough to confirm).
5. **If they get it wrong:** nobody scores. No penalty — just an opportunity to share the real answer.
6. **Highest combined score wins.** (You're both winning either way — you're learning.)

## Make Your Own Love Map

Sit down together and interview each other. Take turns. Be genuinely curious — this isn't an interrogation, it's an exploration. Fill in the categories below for your partner.

### The Cast of Characters

- **Close friends:** Who are the most important people in your partner's life right now?
- **Potential friends:** Is there anyone your partner has been wanting to get closer to?
- **Rivals, competitors, "enemies":** Who causes friction, competition, or stress?

### Recent Important Events

What has happened recently that mattered to your partner? Think beyond the obvious — small moments count too.

### Upcoming Events

- What is your partner **looking forward to?**
- What is your partner **dreading?**

### Current Stresses

What pressures is your partner under right now? Work, health, family, finances, anything.

### Current Worries

What keeps your partner up at night? What are they anxious about?

### Hopes and Aspirations

What does your partner want for the future? Short-term and long-term — career goals, personal dreams, things they want to experience.

## "Who Am I?" Questionnaire

This is a self-exploration exercise. You're not filling this out about your partner — you're filling it out about *yourself*, then sharing it. The goal is to give your partner a deeper window into your inner world.

Take your time. Write freely. There are no wrong answers.

### 1. My Triumphs and Strivings

- What are you most proud of in your life?
- What achievements mean the most to you?
- What difficult periods have you survived? How did you get through them?
- What are your proudest moments?
- What goals are you still striving toward?

### 2. My Injuries and Healing

- What have been the most difficult periods of your life?
- What losses have you experienced?
- What disappointments have you faced?
- How have you coped? What helped you heal?
- Are there wounds that are still healing?

### 3. My Emotional World

- How were emotions handled in your family growing up?
- How did your parents express love? Anger? Sadness? Fear?
- How do *you* express those emotions now?
- What emotions are easy for you? Which are hard?
- What do you need from your partner when you're feeling emotional?

### 4. My Mission and Legacy

- What do you feel your mission in life is?
- What do you want to be remembered for?
- What kind of impact do you want to have on the world, your community, your family?

### 5. Who I Want to Become

- Describe the person you want to be in five years.
- What would you change about yourself if you could?
- What dreams do you still hold onto?
- What new things do you want to learn or experience?

## Putting It Into Practice

You don't need to do all of these exercises in one sitting. The point is to build the *habit* of staying curious about your partner. Pick one exercise to start. Revisit the others over time.

The love map isn't something you complete — it's something you keep updating. Ask questions over dinner. Notice what your partner is stressed about. Remember the small things. That's how you stay connected.
